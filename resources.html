TSA Webmaster requirements checklist

  • Addresses the annual theme: an interactive AI learning portal for grades 9–12 (see the design brief table).
  • Hosted on a web server and available 24/7. The entry URL opens the main page with no password or login.
  • Built by hand with HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No website builders or content management systems.
  • No web framework is used. If one were used it would be documented here.
  • All sources of information are listed on this page.
  • All images, icons, badges and the mascot are original artwork, so no third-party permissions are needed.
  • Designed to work in the latest versions of Microsoft Edge, Firefox, Chrome and Safari, and on mobile devices.
  • All pages were developed during the current school year.

Research sources (Works Cited)

These sources shaped the curriculum, learning experience and design. Entries follow MLA format.

  • AI4K12 Initiative. “Grade Band Progression Charts.” AI4K12, ai4k12.org/gradeband-progression-charts. Accessed 20 Sept. 2026.
    Used for the Five Big Ideas in AI (Perception, Representation & Reasoning, Learning, Natural Interaction, Societal Impact), which organize Level 1 and the curriculum alignment table.
  • Common Sense Media. “AI Literacy Curriculum for Grades 9–12.” Common Sense Education, www.commonsense.org/education/digital-literacy/grades-9-12/ai-literacy. Accessed 20 Sept. 2026.
    Informed the everyday, high-school-level scenarios about privacy, misinformation and online behavior.
  • Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, and European Commission. Empowering Learners for the Age of AI: An AI Literacy Framework for Primary and Secondary Education. OECD Publishing, June 2026, www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/empowering-learners-for-the-age-of-ai_2f8315e7/65cd27d4-en.pdf. Accessed 20 Sept. 2026.
    Shaped our goal of teaching how AI works, how to evaluate its outputs, and how to use it ethically and creatively.
  • UNESCO. “AI Competency Framework for Students.” UNESCO, www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-competency-framework-students?hub=195885. Accessed 20 Sept. 2026.
    Used for the four competency dimensions and the Understand → Apply → Create progression that guided how the four levels are sequenced.
  • World Economic Forum. “Why AI Literacy Is Now a Core Competency in Education.” World Economic Forum, May 2025, www.weforum.org/stories/2025/05/why-ai-literacy-is-now-a-core-competency-in-education/. Accessed 20 Sept. 2026.
    Supports the rationale that AI literacy is a core skill for students.

Sources for specific facts used in lessons, quizzes and games

  • Buolamwini, Joy, and Timnit Gebru. “Gender Shades: Intersectional Accuracy Disparities in Commercial Gender Classification.” Proceedings of the 1st Conference on Fairness, Accountability and Transparency, PMLR, vol. 81, 2018, pp. 77–91, proceedings.mlr.press/v81/buolamwini18a.html. Accessed 24 Nov. 2026.
    Level 2: research showing some face-analysis systems were less accurate for some groups, partly due to unbalanced training data.
  • Modern Language Association. “How Do I Cite Generative AI in MLA Style?” MLA Style Center, style.mla.org/citing-generative-ai/. Accessed 24 Nov. 2026.
    Level 4: style guides now include formats for citing AI tools.
  • McAdoo, Timothy, et al. “Citing Generative AI in APA Style: Part 1—Reference Formats.” APA Style, American Psychological Association, 9 Sept. 2025, apastyle.apa.org/blog/cite-generative-ai-references. Accessed 24 Nov. 2026.
    Level 4: APA also offers formats for citing AI tools.
  • NASA. “China’s Wall Less Great in View from Space.” NASA, 9 May 2005, www.nasa.gov/vision/space/workinginspace/great_wall.html. Accessed 24 Nov. 2026.
    Fact-checking examples: the Great Wall is generally not visible to the unaided eye from low Earth orbit.
  • National Weather Service. “Lightning Myths and Facts.” National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-myths. Accessed 24 Nov. 2026.
    Fact or Fib game: lightning can strike the same place more than once.
  • NASA Space Place. “What Causes the Seasons?” NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory, spaceplace.nasa.gov/seasons/en. Accessed 24 Nov. 2026.
    Fact or Fib game: seasons are caused by Earth’s tilt, not its distance from the Sun.
  • National Geographic Society. “Not All Deserts Are Sandy.” National Geographic Education, education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/deserts. Accessed 24 Nov. 2026.
    Fact or Fib game: Antarctica is the largest desert, and the Sahara is the largest hot desert.
  • “Do People Only Use 10 Percent of Their Brains?” Scientific American, www.scientificamerican.com/article/do-people-only-use-10-percent-of-their-brains. Accessed 24 Nov. 2026.
    Fact or Fib game: we use virtually every part of the brain.

Other basic science facts in games and quizzes (for example, boiling point at sea level, the largest planet, and photosynthesis in chloroplasts) are common knowledge found in standard school textbooks.

Generative AI use disclosure

In keeping with the TSA Use of Generative AI guidelines, we are open about how generative AI was used on this project. Our team used the Claude AI assistant (Anthropic) to help plan the site, draft code and lesson content, and check facts against sources. The team then reviewed, tested and edited the work, made the design and content decisions, and verified every outside fact against the sources listed above. Our Resources and AI Reflection form describes in detail how AI and non-AI tools shaped our choices, what worked well, and what ethical considerations we weighed.

AI Quest teaches the same standard it follows: use AI as a helper, check what it says, be honest about how you used it, and keep your own thinking in charge.
