Quick AI glossary

Artificial intelligence (AI)

Technology that lets computers do tasks that normally need human intelligence, like recognizing speech or images, making predictions, or generating content.

Machine learning (ML)

A way of building AI where a computer finds patterns in examples (data) instead of only following hand-written rules.

Data

The examples an AI learns from, such as photos, text, numbers or sounds.

Generative AI

AI that creates new content, such as text, images, audio or code, based on patterns it learned.

Large language model (LLM)

The kind of AI behind many chatbots. It learned from huge amounts of text to predict what words are likely to come next.

Narrow AI

AI that is good at specific tasks only. All AI available today is narrow AI.

Prompt

The instruction or question you give an AI tool.

Hallucination

When an AI produces information that sounds believable but is false or made up, such as a source that doesn’t exist.

Bias

Results that are systematically unfair or skewed toward or against certain people or ideas, often because of unbalanced data.

Academic integrity

Being honest about your work: doing your own thinking, following the rules, and giving credit for help and sources.

3-Check Rule

Source check, second-source check, sense check. A routine for verifying AI answers.

R.E.A.L. Check

Rules, Effort, Accuracy, Look out. Four questions to ask before you submit or share anything made with AI.
